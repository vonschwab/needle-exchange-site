The Needle Exchange

A music news feed and discovery shelf built from what you actually listen to. It tells you when the artists you play put something out, shows you artists beside your listening that you haven’t heard yet, and says why each thing is there.

Updated 2026-10-13 Screens are the working app, on the author’s own listening
One

What it is

Start here

Streaming services had the right idea: use what someone really listens to to work out what they would want next. Then the idea was corrupted — paid playlist slots, rankings nobody can inspect, AI filler. This is the same idea without any of that. It reads your listening, tells you what is new from the artists you play, and shows its working.

It hosts no music and sells none. It sends you to where the music already is.

The shape

Two parts, both in daily use.

Two

Where it gets things

The sources

Three public sources, each read through its own published interface.

YouTube, through its official Data API, is the main one: an artist’s own channel, official audio and music videos, plus live-session channels. MusicBrainz and ListenBrainz say which releases are new and make sure a name means the one artist. Last.fm is where your listening comes from.

What it does

  • Finds new videos on the YouTube channels of artists a listener plays, and new live sessions on session channels, and tells that listener when one lands.
  • Finds new releases in ListenBrainz’s public list of fresh releases, and checks each against MusicBrainz before anyone sees it.
  • Plays a video through YouTube’s own embedded player, and links to YouTube.
  • Reads each followed channel once for everybody, not once per listener.

What it does not do

  • Host, copy or re-stream any audio or video.
  • Sell music, or take a cut of a sale.
  • Show ads, or sell a place in anyone’s feed.
  • Train any model on what it reads. It trains no models at all.
  • Show YouTube Shorts: they are refused by their vertical format.
Three

Every card says why

The rule

If the app cannot say why something reached you, it does not reach you.

Each card carries the reason in a plain sentence, put together from records the app actually holds: how often you played the artist, over how long, which label you keep coming back to. If a fact is not in the data, that part of the sentence does not appear.

Four

The AI question

Stated exactly

What a language model does here

A model is used in a small number of places, as a librarian. It answers narrow questions about things already found: is this label’s catalogue curated, are these two artist credits the same act, and which known release is this candidate. Each answer is a typed verdict attached to its evidence, and each can be overruled by hand.

It never writes anything you read. Every sentence on a card is assembled from templates over stored facts. It never finds a release; a plain program reading a feed or a channel does that. It never ranks or recommends. It makes no music and no images.

Tests check that the code that polls sources never imports the model module. That wall is the thing I least want to see erode, so I made it noisy.

Five

What it will never do

The rules
✕
No thumbs, no stars, no “more like this.”

Only listening decides what the app thinks you like. Hiding a record or muting an artist changes what is shown, never what it believes about your taste.

✕
No black box, and nothing placed for money.

The ranking measures your own listening: how much you have played an artist, how recently and how consistently. Nobody can pay to appear.

✕
No writing by a model.

No generated descriptions, summaries or headlines, and no invented comparisons between bands.

✕
No social-media machinery.

No chat, no follower or friend counts, no streaks, no public feed, and no way to see whether someone opened or played what you sent.

Six

What it reads, and why

The data

Every field the app takes from YouTube, ListenBrainz, MusicBrainz and Last.fm, how it is taken, why, and what it feeds.

Also read

Two other things come in. A listening-history file the listener exports from a streaming service and uploads, which is deleted after its plays are read. And covers, from public sources such as the Cover Art Archive and YouTube thumbnails: the app stores the link, never the image.

Seven

If you are invited

Joining

There is no sign-up form. I add people I know, one at a time, by invitation link.

1

Open your invitation

An emailed invitation and a password you choose. That is all sign-up asks for.

2

Connect your listening

Give it your Last.fm username, or upload a listening-history file. Nothing else is asked of you.

3

Let it read

It reads your history in the background. Until it has, the page says it is still learning your listening.

After that you can hide a record or an artist, save a record to listen to later, and change your connections in Settings. There is nothing to rate, and no way to follow anyone: what it watches for you comes from what you play.

Eight

Where it stands

Honestly

It is a small, invitation-only alpha. It is not open to the public and is not yet hosted anywhere public.
